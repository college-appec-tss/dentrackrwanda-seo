How to Track Customer Debts in a Small Business

A practical introduction to recording credit sales, payments and remaining balances.

When a business allows a customer to pay later, it creates a debt that should be recorded clearly. A consistent debt tracking process can help the business understand which amounts have been paid and which remain outstanding.

1. Record every credit sale

Write down the transaction when the customer receives goods or services without paying the full amount immediately. Include the transaction date, customer reference, description and amount owed.

2. Keep customer records organised

Use a consistent way to identify each customer. Avoid relying only on memory or loose pieces of paper that can become difficult to find.

3. Record every payment

When a customer pays, record the date and amount. If the customer pays in instalments, record each payment separately so the history remains clear.

4. Review the outstanding balance

A simple calculation is: outstanding balance = original amount owed − total payments received. Check that the recorded payments match the money received.

5. Review records regularly

Set a routine for checking customer balances. Correct errors as soon as they are identified and keep appropriate supporting records.

6. Protect customer information

Customer debt records can contain private information. Limit access to people who need it and avoid sharing customer details publicly.
